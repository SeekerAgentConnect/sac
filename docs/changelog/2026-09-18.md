# 2026-09-18

## Added

- **The Go Prediction publisher template (SEE-96 / Stage 7.1).** The second template, in the same module as the first: [`publisher/cmd/prediction`](../../publisher/cmd/prediction) reads Jupiter's prediction listing, applies the filters its operator configured, and publishes one proposal per market that matches — once, to the shared gateway. Every subscribed phone reads the same document, and each owner then chooses a side and a stake on their own device and places the order through the bundled `jupiter.prediction` plugin (SEE-94). **There is no model in it**: no YES/NO recommendation, no probability of its own, no personalised selection, no order on the server, no position watched, no settlement and no payout. [`docs/wiki/prediction-template.md`](../wiki/prediction-template.md) is the architecture page, including the complete example and what each filter means exactly; [`docs/integrations/jupiter.md#prediction-discovery-see-96`](../integrations/jupiter.md#prediction-discovery-see-96) is the provider as this template reads it; [`docs/development/publisher.md`](../development/publisher.md) is how to run it.
- **The seam held, and that is the news.** SEE-95 said the prediction template would be a second `signals.Kind` over the same core. It is: `cmd/prediction` registers [`signals.Prediction`](../../publisher/internal/signals/prediction.go) and the configuration, the store, the outbox, the drainer, the manifest and the API did not change shape for it. What was added is a provider client and a reconciler; what was taken away is the right to write a signal through the API.
- **Nobody may write a signal on a template that writes its own.** [`api.Authorship`](../../publisher/internal/api/api.go) is `ByCallers` for the CopyTrading template and `ByDiscovery` here, and under the second one `POST /v1/signals`, `PUT /v1/signals/{id}` and `POST /v1/signals/{id}/cancel` answer **403 `written_by_discovery`** — with the reason and the filters named, because a caller's signal would be undone by the next cycle and "my signal disappeared three days later" is a worse answer than "no". `GET /v1/discovery` and `POST /v1/discovery/poll` are added in their place, `GET /v1/status` says `"writable": false` so a client learns it before trying, and the front door does not forward the three either ([`Caddyfile.prediction`](../../publisher/Caddyfile.prediction)).
- **Two filters are the provider's own; the rest are applied to what came back.** The venue and the bucket are parameters, because they decide which events the listing returns at all, and so is the provider's named filter (`new`, `live`, `trending`, `upcoming`). Tags are matched whole and case-insensitively against the event's own; keywords are case-insensitive **substrings** of the event's title, bucket, subcategory and tags together with the market's title; the close-time window is the market's own close time; and the state decides whether a market the provider would not take an order for may be published at all. Every filter is written against a field the retrieved records carry — there is no threshold over data the listing does not send, because that would be a filter that quietly matched nothing. Every market skipped is **counted by reason**, so "my filters match nothing and I do not know which one did it" has an answer in `GET /v1/discovery`.
- **Absence is not closure.** A tracked market missing from a filtered listing may have closed — or may have stopped trending, moved out of the window, or fallen off the pages a cycle read. So it is asked about **directly**, and only the provider's own answer ends a proposal: closed, cancelled, settled, or no such market. A provider that is unreachable, rate limiting or refusing this deployment's key withdraws **nothing at all**, and the cycle is recorded as partial with the problem it ran into. A filter is discovery rather than withdrawal, so a market that stops matching keeps its proposal until the source ends it.
- **Nothing is announced twice.** The expiry is the market's **own close time**, never `now + something`: an expiry derived from the clock would move the revision and wake every subscribed phone on every cycle. A market the provider gives no close time for expires from when this template *first saw* it, which is stored. The idempotency key is **derived** — `market:<venue>:<id>:<generation>` — so a cycle interrupted between reading the listing and storing a signal reaches the same conclusion next time, and the market row's proposal is `UNIQUE`, which says the same thing as a constraint. A cycle every five minutes, a retried publication and a restart all publish nothing and wake nobody.
- **A withdrawal is final, so a re-opened market is a new proposal.** The provider's own rules text describes postponed games, and a market can go from closed back to open. The market row carries a *generation*, which is what makes the new proposal's idempotency key different from the withdrawn one's; the withdrawn document stays readable as cancelled, because a phone that acted on it has its own record either way.
- **The provider's identifiers are published and its links are not.** The document carries the market and event IDs, which is what lets the phone look the market up for itself; the link to the provider's page is kept in the template's own row and served only through its own API. A URL a publisher chose, arriving on somebody's phone, is the thing the manifest rules exist to prevent. And because a market title is somebody else's text, the note is folded to one printable line and cut on a character boundary before anything is published — a title with a newline in it would otherwise become a signal the gateway refuses and nobody ever sees.
- **The provider is read in one place, and its host is named in one file.** [`internal/jupiter`](../../publisher/internal/jupiter) is the listing and one market, its pagination, its error codes classified into temporary and settled, and a minimum gap between calls that keeps a deployment inside the keyless allowance — enforced in the client rather than in its callers, because the way to exceed an allowance is to have two places that each think they are the only one calling. Its host is the one address compiled into the module, and a boundary test allows it in that file alone: it is the rule the phone applies to the same constant (`JUPITER_ENDPOINT`). The endpoints that would be *about* somebody — orders, positions, history, profiles — are not in the module at all.
- **The store is six tables now, and an older file is brought forward.** A `market` table (one row per tracked market, with its state, its close time, its generation, the source link and the two instants a round robin needs) and a `discovery` row (the last cycle, not a history). Schema version 2, applied a step at a time, so a CopyTrading deployment's database opens with this build and keeps its signals and its outbox — refusing it, or creating a second file beside it, would both throw away what makes a restart safe.
- **Its own stack, because it is its own publisher.** [`compose.prediction.yaml`](../../publisher/compose.prediction.yaml), [`Caddyfile.prediction`](../../publisher/Caddyfile.prediction) and [`.env.prediction.example`](../../publisher/.env.prediction.example): its own server ID, its own credential from the gateway, its own database, its own port. What it shares is the image — which now carries all three binaries — and the prediction stack names the other one in its entrypoint.
- **`node scripts/capture-jupiter.mjs --events`**, and seven real captured answers under [`publisher/internal/jupiter/testdata`](../../publisher/internal/jupiter/testdata): two pages of a listing, a page past the end, a parameter the provider refuses, an open market, a settled one and one that does not exist. Each is stored with **the request that produced it**, and the tests serve them back by matching a request parameter for parameter — so a change to what this template asks for fails a test instead of quietly testing something else.

## Added

- **Explicit Sandbox and Production modes (SEE-97 / Stage 7.1).** One environment contract across the manifest, both publisher templates, the shared gateway, both client plugins and this phone's own records, and the shape of it is one sentence: **a manifest says which environments a server _serves_; a connection records which one it _keeps_.** Those are different facts in different places, which is what makes a promotion to production something a person did rather than something a document said. [`docs/wiki/environments.md`](../wiki/environments.md) is the page.
- **Sandbox is the real thing minus the signature.** The same live provider read, the same transaction bytes, the same inspection, the same rules — and then no wallet, nothing sent, and no signature or explorer link invented for something that did not happen. A thinner demonstration would demonstrate nothing: the only difference from production is the last step, which is the step that matters. It is recorded as `Simulated`, with the promise it was bound under beside the cluster, and it counts nothing against a daily threshold the owner set on real money — for the same reason devnet play money never does.
- **Which promise a connection keeps is stored on the connection** ([`Connection.environment`](../../android/app/src/main/java/io/github/brrenat/seekervault/connections/Connection.kt)). A feed starts in **sandbox** whenever its publisher serves one, `ConnectionRepository.setEnvironment` is the owner's only way to move it and refuses an environment the server does not serve, and a manifest that stops naming the one a connection keeps makes that server *unsupported* — readable, executing nothing — rather than moving the connection to the other one. `ConnectionStore` is at version 3 for it, and an older file reads as production, which is what every connection was before the field existed.
- **A direct connection is always production**, by an invariant stated where a connection is built rather than checked where one is used. A rehearsal is possible only where nobody is waiting for the answer: an agent that asked a paired sidecar for a signature can be told no, but it cannot be handed a simulation, and this app will not invent one for it. The private workflow is therefore untouched in both directions.
- **The gateway refuses a manifest that changes what a server promises** (`GATEWAY_PROBLEM_OTHER_ENVIRONMENT`, [`internal/rules/manifest.go`](../../broadcast/internal/rules/manifest.go)). A promise a higher revision can raise is not a promise: every subscribed phone caches a manifest by revision, so one document would move all of them from a demonstration to real money without anybody looking at it. The order the environments are written in is not part of the promise, so a reordered document is a retry. A second environment is a second deployment — which is the rule the publisher's own database stamp already kept at its end.
- **The promise is pinned in the binding and checked inside the wallet's own lock.** [`ExecutionBinding.environment`](../../android/app/src/main/java/io/github/brrenat/seekervault/proposals/ProposalBinding.kt) joins the revision, the choice, the plugin, the wallet and the content hash, and `bindingProblem` answers `other_environment` when it is not the connection's current one — beside the preparation's expiry and the wallet selection, on the far side of the wait. A mode switch that lands while an approval is in flight is refused there; it cannot land between the check and the signature, because there is no gap. And a switch under an open review throws the preparation away on screen, so the owner prepares again and acknowledges again.
- **The sandbox branch has no wallet session in scope.** In [`OperationViewModel.go`](../../android/app/src/main/java/io/github/brrenat/seekervault/operations/OperationViewModel.kt) it is lexically outside `wallet.withWallet`, so a rehearsal cannot reach a wallet by construction rather than by a flag beside one — and it runs the same gate, so it is refused by exactly the things that would refuse the real operation.
- **`publisher/internal/environment`**: which promise a deployment keeps, as one shared type instead of four places comparing string literals. `Parse` is the only way to get one, so an environment that came from nowhere publishes itself as *unspecified* — which the gateway refuses — rather than as production, and a contract test holds the two words against the phone's own `PluginEnvironment` by reading `ActionPlugin.kt`.
- **Both `.env` examples ship as sandbox**, so copying one and running it demonstrates the whole path without anybody's money. Production is a deliberate edit of that line; the code still has no default at all, and a deployment that says nothing does not start.

## Changed

- **The two Jupiter plugins stopped reading the environment.** They used to refuse sandbox before the first network call, and SEE-93 and SEE-94 both said SEE-97 owned what sandbox grew into: this is it. A plugin prepares identically in both environments, because whether the bytes are signed is core's business — core holds the wallet — exactly as which cluster they are for has always been core's. `PLUGIN_CONTRACT` is still **1**, and that is the same decision rather than a lapse of it: nothing about the interface changed, only what core does with a preparation made in one of the environments the plugin already declared.
- **`serverSupport` takes the connection's environment**, and the four places that used to default to `PluginEnvironment.Production` — `ConnectionsViewModel`, `InboxViewModel`, `ProposalRepository`, `OperationViewModel` — now read it off the connection instead. That is the "inconsistent flags" the ticket named, removed rather than multiplied.
- **`StageBoundaryTest` tells a promise from a plugin.** Six core files import `PluginEnvironment` now, and the check that enumerates which files know the registry exists would have simply grown by six. Instead it distinguishes them: a file that imports *only* `PluginEnvironment` knows what a sandbox is and still nothing about a registry, and both lists are pinned — so `PluginRegistry` appearing in `sync/` still fails.
- **`GET /v1/manifest` renders `environments` from the document it already built** rather than from the setting beside it. One fact, one source, and the source is the one a phone will read.

## Added

- **A server deployment with the live update stream.** [`deploy/server/`](../../deploy/server/README.md) runs the sidecar alone on its own TLS listener — the one layout in which `UpdateService.Subscribe`, gRPC over HTTP/2 end to end, reaches the sidecar — with nothing in front of it that speaks HTTP.
