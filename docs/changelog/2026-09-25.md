# 2026-09-25

## Changed

- **SEE-158: the prediction review sheet now matches the design.** A `prediction.buy` signal is
  reviewed in the design's review sheet instead of the older single column. The title is the kind
  ("Prediction") and the source is a chip; the headline is the market, then "5 USDC on Yes" once a
  side and stake are chosen; the header and the Approve/Dismiss footer are pinned and only the body
  scrolls. The verdict card sits directly after Your part and says "Outside rules · no rules set"
  with a Connection rule chip and a Rules pill that opens the connection's rules stacked on top.
  The old "What the publisher proposed", "What the provider says now", "What is being submitted" and
  "What this phone read" sections become one quoted-terms card and one fact row per value: no
  snake_case keys, no base units (`5000000` → `5 USDC`), no ISO timestamps, every money value with
  its unit, and addresses cut at the middle (8…8) in mono, with the mint, proposal and publisher
  copied on tap. Side and stake are chosen in a stacked sheet whose **Use these** re-quotes; a quote
  that runs out shows a Refresh quote card. The swap wording is gone from predictions: the privacy
  note says "How much to stake", the button says "Approve and stake" ("Simulate the stake" in
  sandbox), and an empty stake says "Enter how much to stake." Approve waits for the warning
  checkbox whenever the verdict is orange.

  On Home, a prediction tile's title ends in an ellipsis instead of being cut through a glyph, its
  sub-line is "CopyTrading · Prediction" rather than the publisher's note, and a signal tile's chip
  is the same verdict its review shows — "Outside rules" when no rules apply, instead of "In rules".
  Details: [docs/wiki/prediction-review.md](../wiki/prediction-review.md).

## Fixed

- **SEE-154: removing a connection left its signals counted on Home and listed in the Inbox.** The
  removed feed's row disappeared from Paired servers and no new signal arrived on it, but the two
  it had already delivered kept "Waiting for you" at 5 and stayed in the Inbox under a source that
  was no longer connected — until the app was restarted, which is when they went.

  Nothing was being retained on purpose. `ConnectionRepository.remove` already deletes what a feed
  read, and `ProposalRepository` already publishes only what a live feed owns; what the screens
  were reading was the list it had published *before* the removal, which is re-derived on the next
  publish or on the next `load`. `OperationViewModel` now applies the same rule where the held
  proposals meet the connections as they are now, so the removal takes effect while the owner is
  watching it: the count excludes them, the Inbox stops listing them, and a review of one of them
  closes. Another feed's signals are untouched. A regression covers all of it — two feeds, one
  removed, then the other.

  No storage format, protocol, or retention rule changed: a feed's proposals still go with the
  feed, and the owner's own Activity still outlives both.

- **SEE-153: library-backed sheets now clear three-button navigation.** The safe-area fix from
  SEE-150 applied only when the app host drew a sheet's chrome. Connection details and the other
  `SheetScaffold` screens draw their own chrome, so their last action kept the gesture-navigation
  position when Android switched to the taller three-button bar. The common pushed-sheet host now
  applies the live navigation-bar inset to both presentation paths while keeping the surface drawn
  to the screen edge. A Robolectric regression changes an already-open library sheet from a 24dp to
  a 48dp inset and verifies its bottom clearance grows by the same 24dp.

- **SEE-152: a direct MCP connection never recovered its update stream after a network
  interruption.** Turning networking off and on left the row on "Couldn't reach the server" for as
  long as the app ran, while unary calls from the same phone kept succeeding; **Retry** and a
  force-stop did not help, and re-pairing — which revokes the connection and cancels the requests
  the server was holding — was the only way back.

  The phone had not lost the server, it had lost the owner that watches it.
  `ForegroundUpdateManager` starts one owner per connection, and only from inside the collector on
  the connection *list*, so an owner that ends while its connection stays paired is never replaced.
  Three things ended one:

  - the 30-second wait for the stream's `ready` was a `withTimeout`, whose
    `TimeoutCancellationException` the owner read as its own cancellation and rethrew. A stream
    that opens and then says nothing is the ordinary shape of a connection coming back through a
    route that has forgotten it;
  - `SynchronizationRepository.synchronize` coalesces callers, and a follower inherited the
    cancellation of a leader whose own caller had gone away — a Retry whose screen closed, a worker
    whose job ended. The owner is one such follower, and its call was not guarded at all;
  - a bare `FAILED_PRECONDITION` was read as "this sidecar is too old" and retired the connection
    for the rest of the session. The sidecar answers that code both for a protocol it will not
    speak and for a snapshot or cursor the phone has to replace; only the transport's own
    classification tells them apart.

  Now a cancellation raised inside the owner is a retryable failure rather than the end of
  ownership, the handshake is a bounded wait, a coalesced caller whose run is abandoned takes the
  lead itself, and only an answer the owner genuinely cannot retry its way out of — a revoked
  credential, a protocol the sidecar will not speak — stops it. Recovery starts with the next retry
  after the network returns — the retry delay is capped at 30 s — and what the sidecar stored
  during the outage arrives with the snapshot that follows. That is not a 30-second bound on
  recovery (corrected by SEE-155): the retry that finds the network can still wait out the 30 s
  handshake timeout, and the snapshot and any further retry come after it. Measured on the
  emulator, the stream reopened 10–22 s after the network returned. Six tests cover it, including the reported shape
  end to end: the stream dies, every call fails, calls start working again, the first stream after
  the interruption never answers, and the request published during the outage still arrives.

  No protocol, storage format, or server behaviour changed.

- **SEE-155: PR #66 review follow-up — four findings from the review of the integrated
  `develop` branch.** All four were confirmed by reading the code at `ef3b3f6`; none is a
  regression introduced by SEE-150–154, and none of those fixes was reimplemented.

  - **A publisher stopped checking in for good when its gateway had no `Heartbeat` RPC.**
    `Presence.Run` returned on `unimplemented`, which is correct about a gateway older than
    SEE-150 and wrong about the pair: an operator upgrades the gateway first, and the
    still-running publisher never resumed — so its quiet feed was reported offline for the rest
    of that process's life, and the only remedy was restarting every publisher. The loop now
    retries an unsupported check-in on `publish.UnsupportedBackoff`, doubling from a minute to an
    hour, and says so exactly once per episode and once more when check-ins resume. Explicitly
    permanent refusals — a credential that is not a credential for this server — still stop the
    loop for good, because retrying one cannot help and is how an operator gets locked out.

  - **A thirty-third feed disabled presence for every feed on that gateway.**
    `FeedStatusManager` sent every distinct channel in one request; the gateway refuses more than
    `MostStatusChannels` (32), and the manager caught that refusal and kept its previous map. So
    adding one feed left all of them stale or unknown, on every poll, indefinitely. Reads are now
    split into batches of at most 32 — split, never truncated — and a batch that fails costs only
    its own channels their fresh answer, leaving the other batches and the other gateways to land.

  - **Manual refresh did not refresh feed presence.** `FeedStatusManager.refresh()` existed and
    had a unit test, but nothing in the app called it: the refresh action fetched the connection
    and stopped there. An owner whose publisher had just come back could refresh, watch the fetch
    succeed, and still read "Feed offline" until the periodic poll half a minute later — the one
    situation a person reaches for refresh in. The refresh action now asks for an immediate
    presence read as well, and because that makes two reads of one gateway possible at once, each
    read carries a stamp: an answer overtaken by a later one is dropped rather than published, and
    a feed removed while a read was out is not written back in by the answer already on its way.

  - **And that stamp had two holes of its own, found reviewing the follow-up.** The stamp was
    checked under a lock the write was not, which is a hole only on real threads: on
    `Dispatchers.IO` the periodic read and the refresh are two of them, so the older read could
    pass the check, be descheduled, and write its staler answer after the newer read had already
    published — the exact overwrite the stamp exists to prevent. Claiming the channels and writing
    them are now one hold of the lock, and a regression arranges that interleaving on a real thread
    pool rather than hoping a single-threaded test wanders into it. The stamp also covered a whole
    gateway, although a read does not succeed or fail as a whole: with thirty-three feeds a newer
    read that was answered for thirty-two channels and refused for the thirty-third spoke for that
    thirty-third too, dropping the older read that had actually heard about it and leaving that one
    feed stale for no reason at all. A read now claims only the channels it read, and every other
    channel is still the older read's to answer.

  - **Home called a server unreachable because it did not stream.**
    `ForegroundConnectionState.Unsupported` was part of the `unreachable` condition, so a paired
    MCP server whose unary API was answering perfectly well — which is how the phone learns there
    is no update listener — was shown as "Couldn't reach the server", with a **Retry** control and
    nothing to retry, while the connection's own screen said the accurate thing. Home now reads as
    reachable and names the missing capability in the detail screen's own terms: "No live updates",
    "Upgrade for live updates", "Live updates unavailable". A genuine network failure is still
    unreachable and a revoked pairing still asks the owner to pair again.

  Seventeen focused regressions — fourteen in the app, three in `publisher-support` — fail against
  `ef3b3f6` and pass afterwards, and an eighteenth holds that a refresh which fails outright cannot
  drop the periodic answer still in flight; a `feed-gateway` test pins the 32-channel bound the phone now
  batches to, which the base already had. On the emulator the staking server that does not stream
  now reads "No live updates" on Home; a stopped local publisher reads "Feed offline" while its
  sibling stays online, and a refresh brings it back at once; and the direct stream came back
  after three airplane-mode outages without re-pairing. Push delivery is still blocked on a missing
  Firebase configuration, and the deployed gateway and staking images still predate SEE-150. All
  of it, with timings and evidence, is in
  [`docs/development/emulator-e2e-see155.md`](../development/emulator-e2e-see155.md); the historical
  SEE-151 report is unchanged.

  No protocol or storage format changed. The one server-side change is in `publisher-support`:
  a publisher now keeps retrying check-ins against a gateway without them.
