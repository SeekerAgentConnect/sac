# 2026-09-13

## Added

- **The owner can write a connection's rules (SEE-37 / SAW-027):** a **Rules** screen under each connection, in stock Material 3 and nothing else — switches, checkboxes, radio buttons, chips, text fields, lists, Save and Cancel. Until now Stage 5 could assess a request against a policy that nothing on the phone could create. See [`docs/guides/policies.md`](../guides/policies.md).
  - **A switch per list, because an absent list is not an empty one.** Off is no check at all; on with a list is an allowlist; on with nothing in it allows nothing. Every section says which of the three it is in, in words, under the switch — the distinction the whole model turns on is never left to the shape of a blank control.
  - **Thresholds hang off the asset rows.** One list of assets, each carrying its own per-request and per-day fields, with a separate switch deciding whether that same list also restricts what may move. Written this way, a threshold for an asset the list doesn't allow — `LimitForUnlistedAsset` — is impossible to type rather than an error to report.
  - **SOL is typed in SOL; a token is typed in that mint's own base units.** The phone knows SOL has nine decimal places. It cannot establish a mint's decimal count without a transaction that carries it, and it does not guess: a guess three places out is a threshold out by a factor of a thousand. Erring this way errs strict — typing `10` for ten tokens sets a threshold far below what was meant, which shows up as an unexpected warning rather than an unwanted payment.
  - **Every field shows the exact base-unit number it will store, as it is typed.** The conversion shifts digits and never multiplies, so no floating-point type comes between what was typed and what the chain will see.
  - **Refused in words, under the field it is about:** anything that isn't a plain decimal number (a locale's comma, a sign, an exponent, spaces), a threshold of zero, more decimal places than the asset has, more than `18446744073709551615` base units, a daily threshold below the per-request one, an address that isn't base58 for 32 bytes, and an asset or an address already on the list. Nothing is signalled by colour alone, and the Save button stays off while anything is wrong.
  - **Saving with nothing configured removes the connection's rules.** A stored policy that configures nothing and no policy at all are assessed identically, so nothing is kept. The summary says so before it happens.
  - **Rules this build can't read are never replaced by a blank form opening over them.** The editor says what it found — damaged, from a later version, or naming a rule it has no name for — and offers **Start over from no rules**, which the owner presses on purpose. An empty form saved on top would have deleted rules they set and never saw.
  - **A connection's rules go when the connection does.** `ConnectionRepository.remove` deletes them with the credential, the answers, and the metadata. Rules could be created for the first time in this change, so an orphaned file was possible for the first time in this change.
  - **The summary is the whole policy read back,** not a highlight of it: every list that is a check, every threshold, **which checks nothing covers** — `ALLOWED` is never a statement about a parameter no rule was written for — and the line that never changes, that allowed and under restrictions both need the owner's hand on the wallet.
  - **Whole addresses, wrapped rather than shortened,** and a remove button that names what it removes for a screen reader as well as on screen. The form is laid out with padding rather than fixed heights, and a test drives the whole of it at twice the system text size.
  - **The editor still can't act or speak.** It lives in the policy package, and `StageBoundaryTest` holds that package to a pinned list of reads — now the app's own strings, back button, and date format alongside the connection ID rule, the protocol's requests and networks, the transaction facts, the activity records, and the address rule. Nothing that opens a wallet, a connection, or a socket.
  - Fifty-six new tests: `PolicyDraftTest` (17) for the form and the conversions, `PolicyEditorViewModelTest` (12) for loading, saving, restarting and the unreadable case, `PolicyEditorScreenTest` (22) for the screen, `PolicyActivityTest` (4) for the whole path in the real activity with the app's own storage, and one more on Connection details for the Rules button being reachable however the connection stands. The app's unit suite is 609 tests.

- **The assessment is on the request-review screen (SEE-38 / SAW-028):** what the owner's own rules make of the request they are answering, under everything the phone established for itself and never in place of it. Stage 5's verdict has been computed since SAW-026 and shown nowhere; this is where it is read. See [`docs/guides/policies.md`](../guides/policies.md) and [`docs/security.md`](../security.md#verification-versus-advisory-rules).
  - **The verdict, then every check, in words.** *Matches your rules* or *Outside your rules*, and then each of the six checks with what it read and what became of it — matched, outside the rules, could not be checked, or no rule set. The checks nothing covered are named in a line of their own, because ALLOWED is never a statement about a parameter nobody wrote a rule for. Nothing is said by colour alone.
  - **The line that never changes, under every verdict:** whatever this says, it approves nothing and stops nothing. Both verdicts need the owner's approval here, and their wallet asks again.
  - **Going past a warning is a deliberate step.** A warning that can be tapped straight past teaches people to tap past warnings, so an affirmative answer to a request the assessment warns about waits for a box to be ticked, next to a button that says **Approve despite warnings**. Rejecting never waits for anything: saying no is always one tap.
  - **What the owner agrees to is the assessment, not the request.** The tick is bound to the exact decision it was given for, so a preparation read again, an edited rule, or a moved counter clears it and the reasons are there to be read again.
  - **Having no rules at all is not a warning.** Every request on a phone with no rules is UNDER_RESTRICTIONS for want of any, and asking for a tick on all of them would make the tick a ritual. Rules that are stored and can't be read do ask for one: there the owner wrote something, and this build can't say what.
  - **Read again at the moment of answering, not trusted from the screen.** The rules and the app's own records are read afresh when the request is opened, after every preparation, when the app comes back to the front, and once more as the owner answers. An assessment that changed under them stops the answer — nothing is answered, no wallet is opened, and the review on screen is replaced by the one that stands now.
  - **Input validation still comes first, and is never relabelled.** A preparation that is malformed, disagrees with its request, or couldn't be read whole has no Approve button, whatever the rules made of it, and there is no tick that brings one back. The two are shown apart, in their own words. `docs/security.md` now sets them side by side.
  - **Every program the transaction calls is on screen**, whether or not a rule was written about them — what it would run, next to what could not be verified about it.
  - **The assessment the owner read is kept with the record.** `ReviewedPolicy` on an Activity record holds the verdict's code, the reason codes, the codes of the checks nothing covered, when it was made, and whether they went ahead with a warning in front of them. It is **codes and never rules**: no threshold, no address, and no list is copied into the history. It is carried forward unchanged when a record is written again — a status checked ten times later does not know what the review said and must not take it away — and the record's document version stays 1, because a build that refused a new version would drop the whole record, and a dropped record is a transfer the day's counters never see.
  - **None of it leaves the phone.** No RPC carries a policy or an assessment, and `StageBoundaryTest` now holds the two files that speak to a sidecar to having never heard of one — the single policy import in `connections/` exists to delete a removed connection's rules.
  - Thirty-four new tests across `PolicyReviewScreenTest`, `TransferReviewScreenTest`, `InboxViewModelTest`, `ActivityLogTest`, `ActivityStoreTest`, `ActivityDetailsScreenTest`, `PolicyDecisionTest` and `StageBoundaryTest`, and seven new deliberate breaks, each of which failed exactly the check it should. The app's unit suite is 642 tests.
